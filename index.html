Lightning talks

Short talks on using structure, logic and interpretable models to make reasoning more faithful and checkable, across spatial, legal, medical and time-series settings.

Opening talk · 9:06

When does structure make reasoning trustworthy?

Shreya Rajpal · Michigan State University

When LLMs reason over spatial stories in plain text, they hallucinate relations, and accuracy falls from 93% to 27% as the number of reasoning hops grows. Translating the text into symbolic forms such as relational triples, grids and Prolog coordinates keeps reasoning stable, improves accuracy by 42% and fixes 64% of hallucinations. SATURN applies the same approach to images: it parses 3D scenes and runs LLM-written symbolic programs. The talk ends with an open question about how to check the translation step itself.

Lightning talk 1 of 3

Transforming Interpretability Gains to Explainability Wins

Gokul S Krishnan · Senior Research Scientist, Centre for Responsible AI (CeRAI), IIT Madras

Models that are interpretable by construction, such as Kolmogorov–Arnold Networks (KANs), are getting more accurate. It is still unclear whether that internal transparency gives people better explanations. This talk presents KANEx (MICCAI 2026), which reads saliency maps directly from KAN spline functions for chest X-ray classification instead of approximating them with Grad-CAM. These maps localize better and are more faithful, and grounding a VLM's written explanations in them makes those explanations about 10% more correct. The talk builds on Gokul's work on LExT, a metric for the faithfulness and plausibility of natural-language explanations.

Lightning talk 2 of 3 · EMNLP Findings 2026

LEGO: Synergizing Expert GraphRAG and Expert Chain-of-Thought for Legal Reasoning

Qingjing Chen · PhD candidate, University of Bologna

In law, textual similarity is not legal relevance. LEGO retrieves provisions from an expert-built Civil Law graph of 318 concepts and 409 rule cards linked to over 1,200 Civil Code articles. Its retrieval step picks provisions by how many legal issues they cover, not by surface similarity. A second component, ExpertCoT, then reasons as a legal syllogism: provision, then fact, then conclusion. With an 8B model, LEGO beats GPT-5 and DeepSeek-V3 on multi-hop legal exam questions and stays stable on questions needing four or more hops. Next steps include deontic and defeasible logic and SMT-based verification.

Lightning talk 3 of 3 · EMNLP 2026 Main

Grammar of the Wave: Towards Explainable Multivariate Time Series Event Detection via Neuro-Symbolic VLM Agents

Sky Chenwei Wan · PhD student, SLB AI Lab & Télécom Paris, France

Experts often describe events in sensor data in words, for example "pressure recovers slightly while volume stays stable", because dense labels are expensive to collect. SELA compiles these descriptions into Event Logic Trees built on Allen's interval algebra. VLM agents then find each primitive pattern in the signals and combine the evidence under temporal constraints. Detection works zero-shot, and every detection comes with an evidence tree an expert can inspect. Removing the logic structure costs about 21 points of strict-localization F1.
